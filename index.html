Water chemistry

Water is 98%+ of the cup. Target roughly 50–150 ppm total dissolved solids with some hardness (magnesium pulls flavor) and moderate alkalinity (buffer ≈ 40 ppm as CaCO₃). Distilled water brews flat; very hard water brews dull and scales your kettle. Bottled water in the SCA range, or a remineralization recipe like "Third Wave Water," is the easy fix.

Off-boil rule of thumb: water dropped from boil loses ~2–3 °C per minute in an open kettle.
Extraction Lab06 / 07

TDS & extraction yield

If you own a refractometer, this is where the numbers live. Extraction yield (EY) is the percentage of the coffee's mass you dissolved; TDS is how concentrated the cup is. The classic targets: EY 18–22%, TDS 1.15–1.45%.

Beverage weight ≈ brew water − about 2× dose (the grounds hold roughly twice their weight in water). The field pre-fills with that estimate; weigh the carafe for real numbers.

Technique & Troubleshooting07 / 07

Agitation, pulses, and fixes

Agitation

Every disturbance of the slurry speeds extraction. A gentle swirl after the bloom and once after the final pour ("Rao spin") levels the bed and knocks grounds off the walls. Stirring extracts harder than swirling — use it deliberately, not habitually. Excess agitation migrates fines downward and can choke the filter.

Pulse vs. continuous

Continuous pours keep the slurry hot and the bed deep — more even, faster. Pulse pours (like the 4:6 method) drop the water level between additions, extending contact time and giving you levers: the first pours shape acidity and sweetness, the later pours shape strength.

The bed tells the truth

After drawdown, read the bed. Flat = even extraction. Deep crater or grounds high on the walls = aggressive pouring. A dome = under-agitated. Stalled puddle = too fine or too many fines.

Sour · thin · grassy

Under-extracted. The water left before finishing the job.

→ Grind finer · raise temp 2–3° · slow the pours · add a pulse

Bitter · dry · harsh

Over-extracted. You kept dissolving past the good stuff.

→ Grind coarser · drop temp 2–3° · reduce agitation · shorten contact

Drawdown too slow

Bed is choking — too fine, too many fines, or over-stirred.

→ Coarsen one notch · swirl instead of stir · check burr alignment

Drawdown too fast

Water is channeling through, not extracting.

→ Grind finer · pour lower and slower · wet the filter fully first

Weak but not sour

Extraction is fine; the cup is just dilute.

→ Tighten the ratio (1:15) · keep grind and temp as-is

Strong but muddled

Concentrated and over-extracted at once — common with stale or dark coffee.

→ Loosen the ratio (1:17) · coarsen · consider fresher beans
